Rebuilding a team while it was already running.

I joined O3 at the height of the Great Resignation. As I was backfilling positions, I was also building the operating model: what each role was responsible for, career pathing, how we collaborate, and how we deliver excellent work.

Organization
O3, a 40+ person digital experience consultancy
My role
Senior leadership team. From Director to SVP to Chief Experience Officer.
Scope
Org design, career pathing, leadership development, goals + performance

The problem

Everyone had a different definition of good.

The team was turning over, and nothing about how the design function worked was written down. Expectations lived in people's heads, and people were leaving with them. Designers couldn't say what "senior" meant here, and clients got great work from some teams and uneven work from others.

The solution

Hire and build at the same time.

Pausing hiring wasn't an option, and hiring without a system would have locked in every bad habit. So the operating model got built alongside the backfill: a skills matrix, a department charter, a leadership playbook and a goals framework.

The results

A team that knew what good looked like.

I rebuilt the Experience Team with people hired against a clear ladder, and the leadership frameworks I developed went firm-wide, well beyond the design team.

90%

Retention over five years, after rebuilding the team at the height of the Great Resignation.

50+

Practice skills anyone could self-assess, from "never done this" to "I could teach it."

88%

Client satisfaction, up from 76%, after rebuilding how the team worked.

What I believe

  • People often grow into what they can see.

    If nobody can say what senior means, people guess, and the good ones go find out somewhere else.

  • Teams buy in when they're part of the process.

    The charter, the ladder and the goals were all written with the team, which is why people actually used them.

  • Excellence shouldn't depend on who's staffed.

    Clients notice the gap between your best team and your average one. A shared charter closes it.

What I delivered

Four things we built together.

A skills matrix, a department charter, a leadership playbook and a goals framework. Each one fed the next.

13 roles across research, design and writing, with two ways up, and company values written into every level. One designer used it to decide between leading people and leading projects, and chose projects, confident it was right for her.

Every growth plan was built with the person it belonged to. We defined what makes a great leader, both rated them against the skills matrix, agreed on what to focus on, and mapped quarterly goals with the resources and people to help.
